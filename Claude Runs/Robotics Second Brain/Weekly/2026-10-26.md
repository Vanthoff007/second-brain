## Summary
The most useful item for embedded perception was a vendor-independent Jetson AGX Orin depth-estimation benchmark (NODAR, 2026-10-19), which reports stereo (NVIDIA ESS, 0.70% AbsRel in 8.6 ms) far ahead of monocular metric depth on accuracy-per-millisecond [measured, TensorRT FP16]. On the SLAM side, self-supervised inertial odometry (SSIO) and a Basalt-derived monocular VIO on LaMAria stand out. Active week for arXiv; quiet week for Qualcomm RB5/RB-series news (nothing new inside the window), with NVIDIA's Isaac/Jetson Thor announcement (2026-10-21) the main industry event.

## Worth digging into
1. **Jetson AGX Orin depth benchmark** ([2610.27199](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.27199)): gives measured accuracy/ms/energy to pick a depth front end for a VIO or mapping pipeline; the authors released code (`github.com/nodarhub/orin-depth`, per the paper). Caveat: Orin, not RB5, and Middlebury indoor-style data — transfer to RB5 budgets is an [analysis] gap.
2. **SSIO** ([2610.25988](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.25988)): removes the ground-truth requirement for learned inertial odometry, which matters for adapting IMU models to a new platform's sensor fusion stack.
3. **AnchorFlow-VIO** ([2610.26210](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26210)): small open mono-VIO baseline for edge-class compute; check whether a fixed configuration holds up outside LaMAria [hypothesis, untested].
