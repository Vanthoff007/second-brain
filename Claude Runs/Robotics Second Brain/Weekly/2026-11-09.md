## Summary
The most relevant item for embedded perception is KAIST's reflection-robust stereo VIO front-end (2611.05938): a scalar Kalman-filtered ground-height estimate rejects below-floor virtual features, adding 4.93 ms per VIO update and moving VINS-Fusion from 15.0 Hz to 14.7 Hz [measured, desktop i9 + RTX 4080 laptop GPU, not embedded]. The research side was active (many VIO, loop-closure and fusion preprints); the industry and hardware side was quiet, with no verified in-window announcements for Jetson, Qualcomm RB-series or Raspberry Pi.

## Worth digging into
1. **2611.05938 (reflection-robust VIO front-end):** directly applicable to stereo VIO on polished indoor floors; the 4.93 ms filter cost is cheap, but the 8.96 ms GCNet-S segmenter [measured, desktop GPU] is the part to budget on RB5/Orin-class hardware [analysis].
2. **2611.06308 + 2611.06378 (scale anchoring, unified loop closure):** both target loop-closure cost/robustness; check whether a single-model loop-closure stage fits an embedded compute budget [hypothesis, untested].
3. **2611.04397 + 2611.04912 (extrinsic audit, miscalibration detection):** cheap safeguards for any LiDAR-camera fusion stack where extrinsics drift with vibration.
