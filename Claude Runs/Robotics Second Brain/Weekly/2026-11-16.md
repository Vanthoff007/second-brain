## Summary
The most relevant item is an IIT Bombay / IIT Mandi benchmark of RTAB-Map and ORB-SLAM3 in RGB-D mode on a Raspberry Pi 5 (2611.16472): ORB-SLAM3 tracking took 13–45 ms per frame and RTAB-Map odometry 38–74 ms, and only ORB-SLAM3 at quarter resolution stayed inside the 33.3 ms frame interval, where it lost tracking on most sequences [measured, TUM RGB-D, CPU only]. Its second finding matters as much for anyone benchmarking: runs that lose tracking look both faster and more accurate, so time and ATE must be reported with tracking coverage. The week was active on both sides: nine relevant preprints, and in-window hardware news from NXP (drone reference designs with an open SDK) and two Qualcomm Dragonwing IQ-9075 modules.

## Worth digging into
1. **2611.16472 (Pi 5 SLAM benchmark):** gives a reusable protocol for edge SLAM benchmarks (report coverage next to time and ATE; repeat runs because ATE varied up to 27%) and per-stage profiles showing where the CPU time goes, which carries over to RB5-class ARM cores [analysis].
2. **SLAM Dunk:** the gain came from the sensor model, not the front end; time-varying camera calibration and gyroscope scale/misalignment are states an online VIO could estimate too. The authors expect a sliding-window version to work but have not built it [hypothesis, theirs].
3. **NIF-VIO:** a visual–inertial consistency test with early IMU-factor isolation is a cheap guard for any fusion stack whose "world" can move (lifts, vehicles, moving platforms a UAV lands on); tracking averaged about 20.5 ms/frame on a Ryzen 9 laptop CPU [measured], not on embedded hardware.
